In academic philosophy, this paradox is extremely famous under three names :

A. The Münchhausen Trilemma (or of Hans Albert)
To justify an absolute logical truth, one necessarily falls into one of the following three traps :

 Infinite regress : One must prove A by B, then B by C, and so on to infinity.

 Circularity : A proves B, and B proves A (which you talk about : asserting that one has the key to prove the key).

 Arbitrary stopping (or dogma) : One decides to stop at a place by saying "that is true at 100%", without being able to demonstrate it externally.

B. The problem of the criterion (Sextus Empiricus / Skepticism)
How to know if a knowledge is true ? One needs a criterion of truth (your "key of certainty"). But how to prove that this criterion is the right one ? One needs another criterion to test the first criterion... and so on.

C. The problem of the regression of rules (Wittgenstein)
Lewis Carroll (the author of Alice in Wonderland, who was also a logician) wrote a famous paradox : What the Tortoise Said to Achilles. It shows that to apply a logical rule, one needs a rule that explains how to apply the rule, which creates a logical impossibility to act while remaining purely formal.
